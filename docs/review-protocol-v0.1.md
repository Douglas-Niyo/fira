# Independent review protocol and reviewer study, v0.1 — DRAFT (November 21, 2026)

FIRA only produces a list. The compensating control is the **independent review** of that
list. This document defines that review and how it will be tested with intended reviewers.

## Part 1. The independent review

### 1.1 Who reviews

- The reviewer must be **independent of the person who processed the transactions**: able
  to question that person and not responsible for entering, approving or paying the items
  under review.
- Typical reviewers in a small entity: the council or board treasurer, a finance-committee
  member, the mayor or board chair (where they do not process payments), or an outside
  accountant engaged for the review.
- The clerk-treasurer who processes payments **cannot** review their own items. If no
  independent person is available, the entity does not yet have this control, and the
  review pack should say so.

### 1.2 What the reviewer examines

| Item type | Evidence to look at |
| --- | --- |
| Payment after a bank-detail change | The change request; confirmation by phone to the supplier at a number from an earlier invoice or contract (not from the request) |
| Supplier set up and paid by one user | Supplier set-up documents (tax form, address, contract); the invoices and proof of goods or services received |
| Invoice paid more than once | Bank statement showing whether both payments cleared; any refund or credit |
| Payment with no invoice, or to an inactive supplier | Supporting documents and who authorized the payment |
| Possible duplicate bill | Both invoices side by side; delivery or service records |
| Possible split purchase | Whether the invoices relate to one purchase; the approval rules that applied |
| Monthly self-approval summary | Whether a separate, documented review of those transactions exists |

### 1.3 When to escalate

Escalate to the governing body (and, where required, the external auditor or state
auditor) when any of these holds:

- a bank change cannot be confirmed with the supplier;
- supporting documents are missing or do not match;
- a duplicate or overpayment has cleared and no refund is in progress;
- the person who processed the item cannot explain it;
- the same concern recurs in a later month.

### 1.4 How the outcome is recorded

For every item: reviewer, date, evidence examined, outcome (**cleared**, **corrected** or
**escalated**), and a short note. The record is kept with the monthly review pack and is
itself available to the external auditor.

## Part 2. Reviewer study (planned; not started)

### 2.1 Purpose

To test whether intended reviewers can work with FIRA: whether the review pack is faster
and at least as accurate as the raw alert list, and whether reviewers notice the
important items.

### 2.2 Design

- **Participants:** 6 to 10 intended reviewers (board or finance-committee members, small-
  entity finance staff, or accountants who serve small public entities).
- **Materials:** two comparable sets of synthetic cases (Set A and Set B) with the same mix
  of planted types and innocent noise.
- **Formats:** raw alert list and review pack.
- **Crossover:** each participant reviews one set in each format. The order of format and
  set is varied (A-raw then B-pack; B-raw then A-pack; A-pack then B-raw; B-pack then
  A-raw) to reduce practice effects.

### 2.3 Measures

| Measure | How |
| --- | --- |
| Review time | Total time, including time spent opening supporting details |
| Correct escalation | Planted items escalated; innocent items not escalated |
| Missed issues | Planted items not escalated |
| Understanding | Short questions about what each item means and what evidence to check |
| Perceived workload | Short rating after each format |
| Comments | What was unclear or missing |

### 2.4 Conditions before it starts

- Participation is voluntary, with informed consent; no personal or confidential data is
  used, only synthetic cases.
- Ask whether any institution involved requires ethics review.
  this study is permitted under the author's status.
